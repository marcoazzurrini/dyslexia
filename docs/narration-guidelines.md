# Narration guidelines

How to turn an article into narration, based on accessible-publishing guidance and research. This is a targeted review, not a systematic one.

## Principles

1. **Stay faithful to the author.** Narration preserves the author's words and meaning. Permitted changes are making text speakable and describing visuals, not rewriting or adding explanations.
2. **Separate three kinds of change.**
   - _Pronunciation_ makes notation speakable without changing its meaning.
   - _Description_ conveys information that is in a visual but not in the text.
   - _Explanation or paraphrase_ changes the author's teaching. Do not do this automatically.
3. **Check the surrounding text first.** If the prose or caption already conveys a visual's information, add nothing. Decorative images need no description.
4. **Describe meaningful visuals briefly, then in detail if needed.** For diagrams and charts, give the relationships, values, and trends that matter. Never guess at unclear visuals.
5. **Mark additions.** Keep added descriptions clearly distinct from the author's words, like a reader's note.
6. **Keep the original available.** Describing a visual helps someone listening; it does not replace access to the image.
7. **Use established rules for maths.** Speech styles such as MathSpeak and ClearSpeak, and tools such as Speech Rule Engine, already exist. Prefer them to ad hoc rewording.
8. **Code has no settled rule.** Preserve exact code where the article's point depends on it. Do not present a summary as equivalent to the code.
9. **Narrate the whole article.** Leave out only what is not the article, such as menus, sponsor lines, and archive lists.

## What the evidence supports

- Text-to-speech helps reading comprehension for students with reading disabilities, with a modest, variable effect (meta-analysis of 22 studies, effect size 0.35, 95% CI 0.14–0.56).
- Audio support can change reading strategy without improving comprehension. Replay and navigation matter, not only continuous narration.
- No source shows that AI explanations beat access to the original visuals, or that automated image descriptions are reliable.
